🇧🇷 Brazil 2026: Diagnosis and Opportunity

📉 18 Robots per 10,000 Workers

Robot density is the metric that the IFR (International Federation of Robotics) used to compare countries: how many industrial robots there are for every 10,000 workers in manufacturing. Brazil records 18. The global average is 162. The gap is 8x — and that, paradoxically, is the best news for those entering the job market now.

🧭 Why the gap is an opportunity

A country with low density isn’t a country without a robotic future—it’s a country whose adoption curve hasn’t risen yet. Those who position themselves before the curve capture the full growth.

  • •Brazil ranks 53rd globally in robot density.
  • •Robotization is concentrated in automotive and metalworking.
  • •In Brazil's interior, the density is close to zero — open space.

📊 The numbers for Brazil

  • 18 robots per 10,000 workers — Brazil's density.
  • 162 — global average (8x gap).
  • 1.012 — South Korea, the global leader.
  • 53o — Brazil's position in the global density ranking.

💡 Practical Tip

When someone says “Brazil is behind in robotics,” translate it as “Brazil has 8x room to grow.” The optimistic view isn’t naïveté—it’s a career strategy: you want to be in the market that will grow, not the one that’s already saturated.

📈 52% Growth in 2025

Low density doesn’t mean a stagnant market — it means the market is acceleration. Industrial robot installations in Brazil grew 52% in 2025, driven by the automotive, food, and pharmaceutical industries. The problem: the training pipeline isn’t keeping pace.

📊 What's Driving Growth

  • BYD Camaçari (BA) — plant with a high degree of automation.
  • Stellantis Betim (MG) — cobots on an assembly line.
  • Nearshoring — Brazil gaining plants that would otherwise be built in Asia.
  • Reduced IPI for robots in 2025 — tax incentive.

✓ Growing demand

  • ✓Integrators who install and calibrate robotic cells.
  • ✓Change facilitators for teams receiving cobots.
  • ✓Preventive maintenance technicians for robotic fleets.

✗ Missing offering

  • ✗Universities train engineers, not integrators.
  • ✗Almost no change management courses for automation.
  • ✗The training pipeline isn't keeping up with 52% growth.

💡 Practical Tip

52% growth in one year means every new plant needs people who don’t yet exist. The mismatch between demand (fast) and labor supply (slow) is exactly where salaries rise and careers take off.

🏭 SENAI: 23 ISIs, 100,000 per year

Brazil already has the training infrastructure—it just needs to be directed toward the right gaps. The SENAI operates 23 SENAI Innovation Institutes (ISIs) with robotics labs and trains more than 100,000 professionals per year in automation and Industry 4.0. It's the largest technical training network in the Southern Hemisphere.

🏗️ Concept: the infrastructure is already in place

Any large-scale reskilling strategy in Brazil goes through SENAI. The ISIs already have FANUC, ABB, and KUKA robots installed—the challenge isn’t building labs, but designing the right content and training facilitators.

  • •ISIs equipped with FANUC, ABB, and KUKA robots.
  • •Modular courses ranging from 40h to 400h.
  • •Partnerships with Festo and Siemens.

🏆 Robotec Maker 4.0

O Robotec Maker 4.0 and the national SESI/SENAI industrial robotics competition, where students and technicians solve real automation challenges with collaborative robots. It's Brazil's main robotics talent pipeline: participants have 3x greater employability in Industry 4.0.

📊 How it works

  • Categories: programming, integration, and maintenance.
  • 500+ teams in the national finals.
  • WorldSkills awards — gateway to international competition.
  • 3x greater employability among participants.

✓ What Robotec delivers

  • ✓Hands-on experience with cobots, not just theory.
  • ✓Challenges designed with robot manufacturers.
  • ✓Direct networking with the hiring industry.

✗ What it cannot solve on its own

  • ✗Does not cover change management or fleet supervision.
  • ✗Competitive focus, not adult continuing education.
  • ✗Limited reach among young/technical audiences.

⚠️ Structural gaps

There are four critical gaps in Brazil — and each one is a direct career opportunity. The market has no supply for any of the four. Whoever fills them first captures the market.

⚠️ The four gaps with no offerings

  • 1.Autonomous Fleet Supervision — zero "Fleet Supervision" courses in Brazil.
  • 2.Change management for automation — almost nonexistent.
  • 3.ISO 2025 certifications — fewer than 50 ISO 10218-certified professionals in the country.
  • 4.Vertical content by sector (agriculture, pharma, food) — material imported from the US does not apply to the Brazilian context.

📊 The size of the gap

  • 0 Fleet Supervision courses in Brazil.
  • <50 ISO 10218-certified professionals in the country.
  • 4 structural gaps, all with no market offering.

🎯 Concept: gap = open position

Where there is no offering, prices are high and competition is low. Imported content falls short because it ignores tariffs, local ISO standards, and the realities of Brazil's industries. Whoever masters one of these four topics in the national context becomes the reference almost by default.

💸 Import Tariffs

A robot from USD 50 thousand arrives in Brazil costing USD 90–100 thousand — nearly double the international price. The sum of II + IPI + ICMS + PIS/COFINS doubles the acquisition cost. This extends the automation ROI, but also increases the value of the professional who optimizes operations and reduces the payback period.

📊 The cost of the imported robot

International price ............ USD 50.000
+ II + IPI + ICMS + PIS/COFINS .. ~USD 40-50.000
= Cost in Brazil ............... USD 90-100.000

✓ How to reduce the cost

  • ✓Ex-tariff: reduction of the rate to 0–2%.
  • ✓Special regimes by state (ICMS).
  • ✓International leasing as an alternative to direct purchase.

✗ What increases the payback period

  • ✗Buying directly without checking the ex-tariff classification.
  • ✗Ignore state ICMS tax incentives.
  • ✗Underuse the robot—double the cost, low utilization.

💡 Practical Tip

Because the robot costs twice as much in Brazil, every idle hour weighs twice as heavily. Professionals who maximize utilization and reduce payback are worth more here than in countries where robots are cheap. The high cost is, ironically, what makes your skills valuable.
